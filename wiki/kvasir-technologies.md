# Kvasir Technologies

**Sector**: Offshore Energy
**Official Site**: https://kvasirtechnologies.com/

## Overview
Kvasir Technologies is a Danish cleantech spin-out from DTU (Technical University of Denmark) that has invented a novel one-step process for converting plant biomass (lignocellulosic agricultural and forestry waste) into a CO2-neutral drop-in biofuel. The biofuel serves as a 1:1 substitute for fossil marine fuel with no engine modifications required — "Change the fuel, not the engine." With >96% GHG emission reduction, Kvasir's solution enables the existing maritime fleet to meet IMO 2030 (40% reduction) and 2050 (50% reduction) targets without vessel replacement.

## Business Model
- **Fuel production**: Produces drop-in biofuel that bolts onto the existing marine fuel supply chain
- **No infrastructure barrier**: No switching costs; handles fossil and biofuel interchangeably — enables seamless transition and incremental decarbonization
- **Cost-competitive**: CO2-neutral fuel at cost parity with fossil fuels
- **Multiple market pathways**: Marine fuel (primary), bio-crude (refinery co-feeding), aviation fuel, value-added chemicals

## Funding
- **€10M Series A** (Jun 2026) — to accelerate commercialization of climate-neutral marine fuel

## Key Technology
- **One-step biomass liquefaction**: Pressurized "cooking" of lignocellulosic biomass in alcohol — simple, scalable process
- **>96% GHG reduction**: Close to fully carbon-neutral; uses only non-edible plant material (100% sustainable)
- **Drop-in compatibility**: Direct 1:1 substitute requiring no engine or infrastructure modification
- **Abundant feedstock**: Agricultural and forestry waste (lignocellulose) is widely available globally

## Markets
1. **Marine Fuel** (primary): Direct drop-in replacement for fossil marine fuel — enables existing fleet compliance
2. **Bio Crude Oil**: Co-feeding into existing refineries for greener outputs
3. **Aviation Fuel**: Selective conversion to climate-neutral jet fuel
4. **Value Added Chemicals**: Aromatic chemicals and bio-plastics

## Cross-Sector Connections
