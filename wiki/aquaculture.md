## Cross-Company Patterns
- **Sensor networks**: Universal need for real-time water quality (temp, O2, salinity, pH)
- **Computer vision**: AI-driven fish health monitoring, biomass estimation, and phenotyping
- **Data platforms**: Cloud-based data management for multi-farm operations
- **Gap**: Interoperability between sensor systems remains a challenge
- **Marine telemetry**: Shared interest in environmental baseline data for regulatory compliance
- **RAS specialization**: ReelData AI targets land-based RAS specifically, complementing open-ocean focus of Innovasea and Aquabyte
- **GenAI/LLM**: Kurma AI's AQUA-7B and AquaChat bring conversational AI to farming operations
- **Biological crop modeling**: Astraeus Digital Oyster/Digital Kelp is a novel approach distinct from camera-based fish monitoring
- **Fisheries AI forecasting**: ORCA brings foundation models to predict environmental shocks (HABs, hypoxia, temperature anomalies) — a shared critical risk across ALL aquaculture operations
- **Kelp/seaweed genomics**: MacroBreed's $11M+ grant-backed approach bridges aquaculture and ocean carbon removal (seaweed carbon sequestration); patented non-reproductive strains address regulatory barriers for ocean farming expansion
- **Marine spatial AI**: Nearview LLC's multi-object detection for buoy/gear identification helps deconflict aquaculture sites with commercial fishing operations — critical as ocean space becomes more contested

## Market Context
- Aquaculture sensors market: $1.8B (2025), projected to $4.2B by 2034 (9.8% CAGR)
- Open-ocean farming requires advanced submersible platforms and real-time monitoring
- Land-based RAS growing rapidly, creating demand for AI automation
- Regulatory pressure driving adoption of environmental monitoring technology
- Shellfish/seaweed mariculture gaining investment; MacroBreed's genomic approach de-risks scaling
- Kelp farming regulatory framework evolving — non-reproductive strains may unlock new zones (PCT/US2024/027903)
- Environmental shock prediction becoming critical as climate variability threatens aquaculture reliability
