# SEA.AI

**Sector**: Maritime Operations & Analytics
**Official Site**: https://www.sea.ai/

## Overview

SEA.AI is an Austrian maritime technology company (founded 2018, formerly OSCAR) specializing in AI-based machine vision systems for object detection, classification, and situational awareness on the water surface. With 45 employees across Austria, France, Portugal, and the USA, SEA.AI builds camera+AI systems that detect objects that escape conventional radar and AIS — unsignalled craft, floating obstacles, buoys, inflatables, kayaks, and persons overboard. The company serves recreational, commercial, security, and defense markets.

## Business Model

**Hardware+software product sales** across four market segments:
- **Watchkeeper** (NEW) — ultra-wide field of view; configurable camera setups
- **Sentry** — commercial and government use; 360° situational awareness with AI-driven real-time detection, collision prevention & security
- **Brain** — AI module that upgrades existing thermal cameras (retrofit business model)
- **Competition** — ocean racing and performance yachts with rotating mast

**Recurring software updates** post-sale (Sailing Software Update V8.0, Jul 2026 — smarter color camera AI, wider usable camera view, cleaner interface).

**Multi-segment go-to-market**: recreational sailing → commercial workboats/ferries/offshore vessels → security (coastal monitoring/SAR) → defense (counter-USV, persistent visual intelligence).

## Context

SEA.AI joins **Orca AI** and **Sea Machines Robotics** in the maritime AI/navigation segment. The distinguishing pattern: **marine computer vision as a sensor layer complementing radar/AIS**. Where Orca AI accumulates the world's largest marine visual dataset (80M+ nm) for bridge crew awareness, and Sea Machines builds full vessel autonomy systems, SEA.AI focuses specifically on the **gap between radar and AIS** — detecting objects that neither conventional system sees. The counter-USV use case (defense) is particularly timely given the proliferation of drone boat threats in 2025-2026 naval conflicts. The Robosys partnership for machine-vision-based autonomous collision avoidance extends SEA.AI's reach from awareness into active control.
