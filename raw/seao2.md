# SeaO2 – Raw Web Extract
**Source:** https://www.seao2.com/

## Overview
SeaO2 provides affordable Giga-ton scalable Carbon Dioxide Removal Technology by leveraging our oceans carbon removal power.

The ocean is a massive sink for CO₂, absorbing over a quarter of all CO₂ emissions. Since the industrial revolution, excess atmospheric CO2 has caused ocean acidification. The concentration of dissolved carbon in the ocean is more than 100 times higher than in the air, making it a powerful source for CO₂ removal.

## Business Model
- **Electrochemical Direct Ocean Capture (DOC)**: Uses only renewable electricity and seawater to extract CO₂ from surface seawater. Removes dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) as gaseous CO₂.
- Higher efficiency than Direct Air Capture (DAC) due to higher CO₂ concentration in seawater.
- Products:
  - **CDR credits**: CO₂ stored in geological formations or mineralized into concrete. High-quality credits.
  - **Utilization (Blue CO2)**: Sell CO₂ as feedstock for food, beverages, packaging, agriculture, green methanol, ethylene, etc.
- Target: Gigaton-scale. Partnerships with European storage providers for first tons.
- Pilot in 2025 validated in North Sea seawater (Netherlands). 2026: standalone pilot with 25 tonnes CO₂ removal per year each (multiple pilots).
- Business: Sell CDR credits for net-zero; sell Green CO₂; storage partner opportunities.
- Recent: Mar 2026 Accelerating in South Holland with Platform Zero. Featured in Economist Impact Mar 2026. Recognised by EC BlueInvest Feb 2026.

## Key Tech
- Electrochemistry + vacuum to alter carbonate equilibrium.
- Removes CO₂ from seawater; returns decarbonized water which re-absorbs atmospheric CO₂.
- No chemicals added, only renewable electricity, membranes, seawater.
- Fights local ocean acidification.
- Safe, no added chemicals, high temp or pressure.

## Marine/Environmental Data Needs
Primary: Real-time and continuous MRV (Monitoring, Reporting, Verification) for crediting verification.
- **Ocean carbonate chemistry**: pH, DIC, alkalinity measurements (as process changes seawater equilibrium).
- Dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) removal quantification.
- Surface seawater CO₂ flux, ocean acidification monitoring before/after deployment (acidification counteraction tracking).
- Salinity, temperature, dissolved oxygen for ecosystem impact baselines.
- Current speed, wave height for platform stability (offshore deployment).
- Environmental baseline surveys + continuous platform telemetry for permitting and credits.
- High need for **marine sensor networks + MRV telemetry** and real-time carbonate chemistry data (pH, alkalinity, DIC depth profiles probably).
- Likely needs autonomous underwater vehicles or fixed moorings for verification.
- Interest in external data services for scaling: satellite validation? ecosystem impact data.
